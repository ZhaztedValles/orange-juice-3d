# Facts: An invented puzzle (kind: puzzle; no real people, places or crimes). The river town of Millbrook, a harvest fair. The baker Mrs Plum shows three prize bakes in her shop window: a cherry pie, a plum tart and a giant honey cake. At two o'clock the church bell rings for the boat race and the whole town runs to the bridge; Mrs Plum leaves the window open. When she comes back the honey cake's plate is empty, crumbs lead off down the street. Constable Pike asks Mrs Plum to keep quiet and tells nobody which bake is missing. Answer: Felix. Nobody had been told which of the three bakes was missing, yet he named the honey cake; only the thief could know. A bee buzzes round Felix as a bonus clue.

Someone robbed the village bakery in broad daylight, and the thief gave themselves away with just two words.
Every autumn, the river town of Millbrook held a harvest fair, and the baker, Mrs Plum, was proud of her window.
In it sat her three prize bakes, a cherry pie, a plum tart and a giant honey cake.
At two o'clock the church bell rang for the boat race, and the whole town ran down to the bridge to cheer.
Mrs Plum ran too, and left the window open.
When she came back, one plate was empty, and a trail of crumbs led away down the street.
Constable Pike asked Mrs Plum to keep quiet, and told nobody which bake was missing.
Then he stopped three people near the shop and asked each one, where were you at two o'clock?
Otto the ferryman said, I was rowing in the race, ask anyone, I came last again.
Greta the painter held up her picture and said, I was on the bridge painting the boats, the paint is still wet.
Felix the clockmaker yawned and said, I was asleep at home all afternoon, and I don't even like honey cake.
The constable smiled, closed his notebook, and walked straight over to one of them.
