# Facts: 21 November 1783, Paris. Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier and François Laurent, the Marquis d'Arlandes, made the first free (untethered) flight by people, in a blue and gold Montgolfier hot-air balloon heated by a straw fire in a brazier, launched from the garden of the Château de la Muette at the edge of Paris (Wikipedia: around 2 pm, in the presence of the king). King Louis XVI had first decided two condemned criminals should fly; Pilâtre de Rozier argued the honour should go to them, and the king allowed it (Wikipedia). The two men stood on a gallery on opposite sides of the balloon. They rose to about 3,000 feet and moved along the Seine (Linda Hall). Bits of burning straw set small patches of the fabric alight, leaving glowing holes; d'Arlandes put them out with a wet sponge on the end of his pitchfork (Linda Hall; the Science Museum says each carried a sponge and a bucket of water). When he paused to do it the balloon sank and Pilâtre shouted at him to feed the fire (Linda Hall). The envelope burned through in several places; d'Arlandes wanted to come down, but Pilâtre pointed out they were over the rooftops of Paris, so they fed the fire and rose again (Science Museum). After crossing the river d'Arlandes let the fire die; they came down gently in a field between two windmills, near today's Place d'Italie (Butte-aux-Cailles), after about 25 minutes and about 5.5 miles (9 km). The collapsing balloon had to be pulled from the embers, and Pilâtre de Rozier, smothered in its folds, was pulled out too (Linda Hall). The countdown stamps (twenty-five, twenty, fifteen, ten, five, one minute) are the flight time left, placed in the order the sources give the events; the sources do not time each event to the minute.

The first two people ever to fly spent their twenty-five minutes in the sky putting out fires.
It was the twenty-first of November, seventeen eighty-three, in a garden at the edge of Paris.
The Montgolfier brothers had built a blue and gold balloon, lifted by the hot air of a straw fire.
The king wanted two prisoners to risk it first, but Pilatre de Rozier and the Marquis d'Arlandes begged for the honour.
Twenty-five minutes to go. They rise, and the crowd falls silent below.
Twenty minutes. The balloon drifts along the river Seine, three thousand feet up.
Fifteen minutes. Bits of burning straw land on the cloth, and small glowing holes open in the balloon.
Ten minutes. D'Arlandes dabs them out with a wet sponge on the end of his pitchfork, and the balloon starts to sink.
Pilatre shouts at him to feed the fire, so the fire grows, and so do the holes.
Five minutes. D'Arlandes wants to land, but they are drifting over the rooftops of Paris.
One minute. They clear the city and let the fire die.
The balloon settles gently into a field between two windmills.
Then the huge cloth collapses on top of them, beside the embers.
The first man in history to fly had to be dragged out from under his own balloon.
