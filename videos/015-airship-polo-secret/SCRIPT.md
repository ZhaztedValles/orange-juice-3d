# Facts: In Paris in June 1903 (Wikipedia: 27 June; other accounts 29 June), Aida de Acosta (1884-1962), a wealthy nineteen-year-old Cuban-American from New York, met the Brazilian aviation pioneer Alberto Santos-Dumont, who flew his small airship No. 9 (the "Baladeuse") around Paris. After about three lessons he let her fly No. 9 alone from Paris (Neuilly) to the Chateau de Bagatelle, while he rode a bicycle below, waving his arms and shouting advice. She came down on the polo field at Bagatelle during a match between American and British teams; spectators helped her out of the basket; she watched the polo and then flew back; the trip took about one and a half hours. Santos-Dumont called her "the first woman aero-driver in the world". She was the first woman to pilot a powered aircraft, about six months before the Wright brothers' first powered aeroplane flight (December 1903). Her parents were horrified: they believed no man would marry a woman who had done such a thing, and Santos-Dumont was asked never to reveal her name. In that era a respectable woman's name appeared in the newspapers only when she was born, married and died. She never flew again and kept the flight secret for about thirty years (29 years in some accounts), until she told it at a dinner in the 1930s, to her husband and a young naval officer, Lieutenant George Calnan, who talked of flying airships.
