# Facts: The Schildbürger are the townsfolk of Schilda, a fictional German town of fools; their tales were first printed anonymously in Strasbourg in 1597 as the Lalebuch and in 1598 as Die Schiltbürger (Wikipedia). In the tale, the men of Schilda were so wise that princes kept calling them away to give counsel until no men were left at home, so the town agreed to cultivate folly instead (Tieck's version, World's Wit and Humor, 1906). They built a new town hall with three walls and forgot the windows: inside it was pitch dark (Tieck; Die Schildbürger, ch. 11 and 14). They tried to carry the daylight in, in long sacks, pots, kettles, tubs, a basket, a shovel and a mousetrap, and it did not work (Die Schildbürger, ch. 12). A travelling stranger had them take the roof off, and they met under the open sky all summer (ch. 13); in winter the roof went back on and it was dark again, until a councillor saw light coming through a small crack in the wall and they realised they had forgotten the windows (ch. 14). In the end their town burned down and they scattered to many places, so foolish folk everywhere are said to descend from them (ch. 47).

Legend says one town was so clever that it became foolish on purpose.
The town was Schilda, in German tales printed in fifteen ninety-seven.
Its men were so wise that princes kept calling them away for advice.
Soon no men were left at home.
So the elders agreed: if wisdom took them away, foolishness would keep them home.
First they built a grand town hall, with three tall walls and a proud roof.
On opening day, the whole town marched inside.
It was completely dark.
Not a speck of light, on a sunny day.
At noon, they caught sunshine in sacks, buckets and even a mousetrap, and ran it indoors.
They poured it out. Still dark.
Then a traveller told them to take the roof off.
Light poured in, and all summer the council met under the sky.
But in winter the roof went back on, and so did the dark.
At last, one councillor saw a thin beam of light through a crack in the wall.
Windows! They had forgotten windows!
Most likely, the tales were just jokes about proud little towns.
But the legend says Schilda played the fool so long that it stuck.
When their town burned down, they scattered all over the world.
And that, the story says, is why every town has a few Schildas.
