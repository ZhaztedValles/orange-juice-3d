# Facts: NASA's Voyager 1 launched on 5 September 1977 from Cape Canaveral, Florida; it is about the size of a small car, and eight of its ten original instruments are no longer operating while its plutonium power source degrades a little each year (UPI / The Conversation, 18 November 2026; Scientific American, 18 November 2026: only two instruments still work, its generators give less than half their launch power). At 5:16 a.m. EST (10:16 GMT) on 18 November 2026 it became the first human-made object to reach one light-day from Earth, about 16 billion miles (26 billion km), more than four times the distance from the Sun to Pluto; a radio signal takes 24 hours each way (UPI; Scientific American). On 14 November the mission team learned that the spacecraft had stopped sending science and engineering data after a Deep Space Network antenna in Canberra, Australia, locked on and received a carrier signal but no data (NASA, 18 November 2026; CNN, 24 November 2026). The carrier means the attitude computer still keeps it pointed at Earth and its antenna and broadcast equipment still work; the lack of data suggests a problem with the onboard computer that collects and formats the data; with 24 hours each way, diagnosing it may take weeks or longer (NASA; CNN). In 2023 Voyager 1 had a similar fault, a carrier signal with no data, caused by one of its onboard computers (NASA). NASA confirmed the milestone from the carrier signal and trajectory calculations and celebrated it on 19 November at JPL in Pasadena, California, a day after it was reached, once the spacecraft's signal had arrived (NASA; Space.com).
