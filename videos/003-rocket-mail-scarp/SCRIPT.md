# Facts: Scarp, a small island off the Isle of Harris, Outer Hebrides, July 1934; the German rocket enthusiast Gerhard Zucker had flown about one thousand letters in test rockets on the Sussex Downs in June 1934; the Scarp test was arranged with the British authorities to send mail across the sound from Scarp to Harris, just under a mile; Scarp had no telephone; the rocket carried about twelve hundred stamped letters (some sources say more); it rose only a few feet and exploded in a flash, dull bang and smoke, and the scorched letters fell like confetti on the beach; 793 letters were salvaged and stamped as damaged by explosions; Zucker blamed poorly packed powder.
Sources: https://cabinetmagazine.org/issues/23/turner2.php, https://www.smithsonianmag.com/daily-planet/department-of-what-were-they-thinking-25283814

In nineteen thirty-four a man tried to post letters by rocket and got a beach full of burnt confetti.
The place is Scarp, a tiny Scottish island with no phone, so mail is slow and news is slower.
Gerhard Zucker, a German rocket fan, has the cure, and he has already flown a thousand letters in England.
Now officials come to watch him fire the island's mail across the sound to Harris, and he swears it will fly in seconds.
He packs over a thousand stamped letters into a rocket and lights the fuse and everyone leans in.
Then comes a flash and a bang and a cloud of smoke and the rocket has barely left the ground.
You see, a log on a campfire burns slowly because flames only lick the outside, but split that log into kindling and the whole pile goes up at once.
Zucker blamed his powder, packed loose, so flame raced through every gap together.
The rocket burst instead of flying and the letters came down like confetti.
Only seven hundred ninety-three were saved and each got a stamp for damage by explosion.
He promised the fastest mail in the world and the beach got the first delivery.
