# Facts: Legend (Chronica regia Coloniensis, first compiled in the 1170s; Grimm, German Legends no. 493): in 1140 King Conrad III (Hohenstaufen) besieged Weinsberg, a stronghold of Welf VI, in what is now Baden-Wuerttemberg, Germany. The town surrendered on 21 December 1140. Conrad granted the women safe conduct with whatever they could carry on their shoulders; they left their goods behind and came down carrying their husbands. Duke Frederick (Grimm: "many of the king's people") objected; the king laughed and said it would not be fitting to change his royal word, and the men were spared. The castle above the town is called Weibertreu ("wifely loyalty"). Historicity is uncertain: the chronicle is the main early source, the Grimms list it among a class of similar legends, and it has been suggested the story was told to show the king's mercy and fidelity to his word.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Weinsberg, https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/legend493.html

Legend says a whole town of women once walked out of a siege carrying their husbands on their backs.
It's December, eleven forty, in Weinsberg in southern Germany, a town proud of its castle on the hill.
King Conrad the Third has camped below the walls with his army, losing patience.
The town has held out for weeks, but now the bread is running out.
The king sends his terms. The men will be punished, but the women may leave in peace.
Each woman may take only what she can carry on her shoulders.
At dawn the gate creaks open, and soldiers wait for sacks of silver and bread.
Instead, every wife comes down the hill bent double, with her husband on her back.
His men shout that it's a trick.
But the king just laughs, and says a king must keep his word.
So the men of Weinsberg walk free, carried every step of the way.
Did it happen? It comes from a chronicle written decades later, and similar tales are told all over Europe.
It may have been spread to make the king look merciful.
But the people of Weinsberg still believe it, and their castle ruin is named Weibertreu, the faithfulness of wives.
The town that never fell was saved by the people who were allowed to leave it.
