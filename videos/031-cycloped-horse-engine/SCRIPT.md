# Facts: The Rainhill trials were run from 6 to 14 October 1829 on a one-mile length of level track at Rainhill in Lancashire, near Liverpool, by the Liverpool and Manchester Railway, with a prize of £500 for the winning locomotive (Wikipedia; Linda Hall Library). Between 10,000 and 15,000 people came to watch (Wikipedia; Linda Hall). Ten engines were entered but only five ran: Rocket, Novelty, Sans Pareil, Perseverance and the Cycloped (Linda Hall; Wikipedia). All were steam engines except the Cycloped, built and patented in 1829 by Thomas Shaw Brandreth of Liverpool, a director of the railway, which was driven by a horse walking on a treadmill platform that turned its four flanged wheels (Science Museum Group; Linda Hall; Wikipedia). With its horse it weighed about 3 tons and reached only about six miles per hour (Science Museum Group). It was the first to drop out, after the horse burst / fell through the floor (Wikipedia; Linda Hall). Rocket, by George and Robert Stephenson, was the only engine to complete the trials, averaging about 12 mph with a top speed of about 30 mph, and won the £500 (Wikipedia; Linda Hall). The railway opened in September 1830 (Wikipedia).

In eighteen twenty-nine, a horse entered a race to find the best steam engine in the world.
That October, ten thousand people crowded beside a mile of track at Rainhill, near Liverpool.
A new railway offered five hundred pounds to the best locomotive.
Four machines hissed with fire and steam.
The fifth was the Cycloped, built by Thomas Brandreth, a director of the railway itself.
Inside it, a horse walked on a moving floor, and the floor turned the wheels.
No boiler, no coal, just one horse.
The crowd laughed, but it really moved, the horse marching bravely in place.
It crept along at about six miles an hour.
Then came a loud crack, and the horse went straight through the floor.
The Cycloped was out.
You see, a horse on a treadmill is like pedalling an exercise bike bolted to a car.
Your legs must move the car, the bike, and you.
With its horse, the Cycloped weighed about three tons.
A steam engine carries its strength as coal, and one sack of coal can do a horse's whole day of work.
The Rocket won, at up to thirty miles an hour.
Within a year, real trains ran to Manchester.
And the Cycloped became the only horse in history to lose a race by falling through the floor.
