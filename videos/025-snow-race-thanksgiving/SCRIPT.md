# Facts: Chicago, Thanksgiving Day, 28 November 1895. The Chicago Times-Herald race, America's first automobile race, organised by publisher H. H. Kohlsaat with a purse of five thousand dollars (first prize two thousand dollars). Eighty-three entries (Wikipedia, Smithsonian), only six started, from Jackson Park (Midway Plaisance) at about 8:55 a.m., on a course of about fifty-four miles to Evanston and back. A storm the night before left about six inches of snow, with drifts, about 30 F (SEP). Starters: Frank Duryea's Duryea Motor Wagon, three Benz cars (Mueller of Decatur, Macy's, De La Vergne) and two electrics (Morris & Salom Electrobat, Sturges). Every car carried an umpire. A Benz collided with a horse tram/streetcar early on (Wikipedia; SEP: the Macy Benz hit a sleigh and a hackney cab). The electrics gave up after about twelve and fifteen miles (Wikipedia). Duryea's steering arm snapped (SEP: on the Rush Street Bridge) and a blacksmith repaired it (Wikipedia: two breakdowns, repaired at a forge). Duryea finished first at 7:18 p.m. after ten hours and twenty-three minutes, about fifty-two miles, averaging about five miles an hour. Oscar Mueller lost consciousness from exposure; his umpire Charles Brady King drove the Benz to the finish (Wikipedia), holding the tiller in one hand and Mueller in the other (SEP), arriving at 8:53 p.m. Only two of the six finished.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Times-Herald_race, https://www.saturdayeveningpost.com/2015/11/the-first-auto-race-thanksgiving-1895/

The first car race in America was won at about five miles an hour, and the car in second place came in with its driver passed out.
Chicago, Thanksgiving Day, eighteen ninety-five.
A newspaper will pay two thousand dollars to the best new motor car that can go up to Evanston and back, and each car must take an umpire on board.
Over eighty men sign up, but the night before a storm buries the streets in snow and just six cars make it to the start.
One slides into a horse tram and two electric cars soon give up.
Then Frank Duryea's steering snaps and a blacksmith has to fix it, so he falls an hour behind.
Oscar Mueller pushes his Benz on through the wind, and his eyes start to close.
The engine is fine, so what is wrong with the man?
You see, moving air takes heat from you much faster than still air, the way you blow on hot soup to cool it, and in an open car each mile is an icy wind in your face.
After ten hours Duryea wins.
Late that night the Benz rolls in with the umpire, Charles King, steering with one hand and holding up Mueller with the other.
So while Chicago ate its turkey, the umpire took second place.
