# Chapter 4: A Whiff of Grapeshot (facts and sources: ../SCRIPT.md)

<!-- narration -->
In the summer of seventeen ninety-four, the leaders of the Revolution turned on each other.
Robespierre, the most powerful man in France, was arrested and executed.
Napoleon had been friends with his younger brother, so Napoleon was arrested too.
He spent less than two weeks in prison, and for a while it looked like his career was over.
He was released, but left in Paris without a command and short of money.
He even thought about leaving France to train the cannon crews of the Ottoman Sultan.
Then, in October seventeen ninety-five, his luck turned again.
Thousands of armed royalists marched on the government in the heart of Paris.
A politician named Paul Barras was given the job of defending it, and he remembered the young general from Toulon.
Napoleon knew exactly what mattered, cannons.
A young cavalry officer named Joachim Murat galloped off to fetch forty guns before the rebels could seize them.
When the royalists attacked near the church of Saint-Roch, the cannons fired, and the attack fell apart within hours.
A later historian called it a whiff of grapeshot.
For Napoleon, it changed everything, and he was given command of the army in Paris.
Around this time, he met Josephine de Beauharnais, an elegant widow six years older than him.
He fell for her hard, and they married in March seventeen ninety-six.
Two days later, he left for his biggest job yet, command of the French army in Italy.
His soldiers were hungry and unpaid, and their boots were falling apart.
He was twenty-six years old.
In a single year, he beat army after army.
At the town of Lodi, in May seventeen ninety-six, his troops charged across a long wooden bridge under heavy fire, and took it.
Afterwards, his veterans started calling him the little corporal.
He came back to Paris a hero.
And the men in power started to wonder if he was becoming too popular.
