As First Consul, Napoleon worked at a furious pace.
He set up a national bank, new high schools called lycees, and a strong central government.
Most famously, he ordered one single set of laws for the whole country.
The Civil Code of eighteen oh four made every man equal before the law, and protected property.
But he also took freedoms away.
Newspapers were shut down or censored, police watched his critics, and the new Code gave husbands power over their wives.
In eighteen oh two, he was made First Consul for life.
Two years later, he went one step further.
The Senate proclaimed him Emperor of the French, and a public vote approved it.
Which brings us back to that cold December day in Notre-Dame.
Historians still argue about whether the Pope knew what was coming.
But the message was clear to everyone watching.
Napoleon owed his crown to no one but himself.
Then he turned, and crowned Josephine as his empress.
From a mocked schoolboy to emperor, in just twenty-five years.
Of course, the story does not end there.
Napoleon's wars would cost millions of lives.
His invasion of Russia in eighteen twelve ended in disaster, and he was finally beaten at Waterloo in eighteen fifteen.
He died in exile, on a remote island in the South Atlantic, in eighteen twenty-one.
And the famous claim that he was tiny?
It is a myth.
He was around five foot seven, quite normal for his time.
The legend grew from British cartoons that drew him as a furious little man, and from that nickname, the little corporal.
But his Civil Code outlived him, and versions of it still shape the laws of many countries today.
The boy from Corsica never stopped believing he was meant for something bigger.
