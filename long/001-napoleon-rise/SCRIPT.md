## Facts (as the sources state them; source numbers in brackets)

1. Corsica was ceded by the Republic of Genoa to France in 1768 (Treaty of Versailles); Corsican resistance under
   Pasquale Paoli was defeated in 1769 (Ponte Novu). [1][2]
2. Napoleone Buonaparte was born on 15 August 1769 in Ajaccio, Corsica, to Carlo Buonaparte, a lawyer of minor
   Corsican nobility, and Letizia Ramolino; eight of their children survived infancy. [1][2]
3. Carlo had supported Paoli, then rallied to the French and won the favour of the French governor, the Comte de
   Marbeuf; this helped Napoleon obtain a royal scholarship for a military school. [1][2]
4. Napoleon left Corsica in December 1778 (aged 9); a few months at Autun to learn French; Brienne military school
   from May 1779 to 1784; mocked for his Corsican accent and poverty; strong in mathematics; read history. [1][2][3]
5. The Brienne snowball-fight story (winter 1783-84) is a widely told anecdote of uncertain accuracy. [2][3]
6. October 1784: École Militaire, Paris; chose artillery. His father died in February 1785 (stomach cancer).
   He completed the course in one year and was commissioned second lieutenant of artillery in September 1785, aged 16.
   He returned to Corsica on leave in September 1786. [1][2]
7. The Revolution began in 1789; Louis XVI was executed in January 1793; France at war with much of Europe; many noble
   officers had emigrated. [1][2]
8. Toulon rose against the Convention in 1793 and admitted a British and Spanish fleet. Captain Bonaparte replaced the
   wounded artillery commander (September 1793), saw that the heights commanding the harbour (Fort Mulgrave, "Little
   Gibraltar") were the key, built batteries close to the enemy (one called the "battery of the men without fear"),
   was wounded in the thigh by a bayonet in the assault of 17 December 1793; the British left within two days; he was
   promoted brigadier general on 22 December 1793, aged 24. [1][2][4]
9. After Robespierre's fall (July 1794), Napoleon, an associate of Augustin Robespierre, was arrested in August 1794 and
   released after about eleven days; in 1795 he asked to go to Turkey to reorganise the Sultan's artillery. [1][2]
10. 13 Vendémiaire (5 October 1795): a royalist insurrection in Paris; Paul Barras gave Napoleon the defence; Joachim
    Murat brought about forty cannon from Sablons; the attack near Saint-Roch was broken; Thomas Carlyle later called
    it "a whiff of grapeshot"; Napoleon was made commander of the Army of the Interior. [1][2][5]
11. He married Joséphine de Beauharnais, a widow born in 1763, on 9 March 1796, and left on 11 March to command the
    poorly supplied Army of Italy, aged 26. Bridge of Lodi, 10 May 1796; afterwards his soldiers called him "le petit
    caporal". [1][2]
12. Egypt: sailed May 1798 with about 35,000 soldiers and some 167 scholars (savants); aim to threaten British trade with
    India; Battle of the Pyramids 21 July 1798 (French infantry squares against Mamluk cavalry); Battle of the Nile,
    Aboukir Bay, 1-3 August 1798: Nelson destroyed or captured almost all the French ships of the line; the failed siege
    of Acre in Syria (1799); the Rosetta Stone found by French soldiers in July 1799, later key to reading
    hieroglyphs. [1][2][6][7]
13. Napoleon left Egypt in August 1799, landed in France in October, and joined the coup of 18-19 Brumaire
    (9-10 November 1799); at Saint-Cloud the Council of Five Hundred shouted him down; his brother Lucien, its president,
    rallied the troops who cleared the hall; Napoleon became First Consul, aged 30. [1][2]
14. As First Consul: Bank of France (1800), lycées (1802), centralised administration, the Civil Code (21 March 1804:
    equality before the law, property; it gave husbands authority over wives); press censorship and police
    surveillance. Consul for life 1802. Proclaimed Emperor by the Senate in May 1804, confirmed by a plebiscite. [1][2]
15. Coronation in Notre-Dame, 2 December 1804, with Pope Pius VII present; Napoleon crowned himself and then crowned
    Joséphine. How far the Pope was told in advance is debated. [1][8][9]
16. The Napoleonic Wars cost millions of lives; the 1812 invasion of Russia failed; defeat at Waterloo (18 June 1815);
    exile on Saint Helena (South Atlantic), where he died on 5 May 1821. [1][2]
17. His height was about 1.69 m (5 ft 7 in), average for the time; the "short" image comes from British caricature and
    the "little corporal" nickname. The Civil Code influenced the civil law of many countries. [1][10]
