# Chapter 1: The Boy Who Crowned Himself (facts and sources: ../SCRIPT.md)

<!-- narration -->
Paris, December the second, eighteen oh four.
Inside the cathedral of Notre-Dame, thousands of guests are waiting, and the Pope himself has travelled all the way from Rome.
But when the big moment comes, the man at the altar does something nobody will forget.
He lifts the crown, and places it on his own head.
His name is Napoleon Bonaparte, and he is thirty-five years old.
Just twenty years earlier, he was a skinny, broke teenager that the other boys laughed at.
So how does an outsider from a small island become the emperor of France?
This is the story of his rise, from a sleepy harbour town to the throne, with all the gambles, lucky breaks and close calls along the way.
It starts on the island of Corsica, in the Mediterranean Sea.
For centuries, Corsica belonged to the Republic of Genoa.
Then, in seventeen sixty-eight, Genoa handed the island over to France.
Many Corsicans did not want to be French, so they fought back, and they lost.
A few months later, on the fifteenth of August seventeen sixty-nine, a baby boy was born in the port town of Ajaccio.
His parents named him Napoleone.
His father, Carlo, was a lawyer from a family of minor nobles, proud of the name but short of money.
His mother, Letizia, ran a strict house, where eight of her children grew up.
Here is the twist.
Carlo had fought against the French, but once the war was lost, he made a smart choice.
He made friends with the new French governor of the island.
And that friendship opened a door that would change everything.
France offered free places at its royal military schools to the sons of poor noble families, and Carlo made sure his boy got one.
At the end of seventeen seventy-eight, nine-year-old Napoleone said goodbye to his sunny island.
He sailed to a country whose language he did not speak, to a school full of rich kids.
He would not see home again for almost eight years.
