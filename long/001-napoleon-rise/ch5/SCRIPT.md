# Chapter 5: Pyramids and a Lost Fleet (facts and sources: ../SCRIPT.md)

<!-- narration -->
The government had an idea that suited everyone, send the popular general far away.
In May seventeen ninety-eight, Napoleon sailed for Egypt with around thirty-five thousand soldiers.
The plan was to threaten Britain's trade route to India.
He also brought something no army had brought before, more than one hundred and sixty scientists, artists and engineers, to study everything they found.
In July, near Cairo, his army met the famous Mamluk cavalry.
In the distance, on the horizon, stood the pyramids.
The French formed huge hollow squares, bristling with muskets, and the charging horsemen broke against them.
The Battle of the Pyramids was a crushing French victory.
But eleven days later, everything went wrong.
The British admiral Horatio Nelson found the French fleet at anchor in Aboukir Bay.
In one night of fighting, almost the entire fleet was destroyed.
Napoleon and his army were now stuck in Egypt, cut off from France.
The campaign dragged on, through desert marches, disease, and a failed siege in Syria.
And yet, in seventeen ninety-nine, French soldiers digging near the town of Rosetta found a slab of dark stone covered in three kinds of writing.
The Rosetta Stone would later help scholars finally read Egyptian hieroglyphs.
Then news arrived from home.
France was losing the war in Europe, and the government was weak and unpopular.
Napoleon made a bold decision.
In August, he slipped away on a small ship, left his army behind, and dodged the British navy all the way back to France.
Instead of being punished, he was cheered in the streets.
Within weeks he joined a plot to overthrow the government.
In November seventeen ninety-nine, the plan nearly failed when angry lawmakers shouted him down.
His brother Lucien saved the day, and soldiers cleared the hall.
Napoleon became First Consul, the most powerful man in France.
He was thirty years old.
