# Chapter 2: The Outsider at School (facts and sources: ../SCRIPT.md)

<!-- narration -->
First, he spent a few months at a school in the town of Autun, just to learn French.
Then, in the spring of seventeen seventy-nine, he arrived at the military school of Brienne, in the cold north-east of France.
It was hard from the very first day.
The other boys came from old French families with money.
Napoleone was small, poor, and spoke with a thick Italian accent.
They mocked his strange name, and they mocked his island.
He did not try to fit in.
Instead, he read, everything about ancient history and the lives of great generals like Alexander and Julius Caesar.
And he was very good at one subject above all the others, mathematics.
There is a famous story from those years.
One winter, so much snow fell that the boys built a fortress of snow in the school yard.
Napoleone took charge of one side, planned the walls, and led a giant snowball battle.
Historians are not sure how much of this story is true, but it was told about him for the rest of his life.
In seventeen eighty-four, at fifteen, he was picked for the top military school in Paris, the Ecole Militaire.
He chose the artillery, the cannons.
It was a branch for clever officers, not for rich ones, where maths mattered more than a famous family name.
Then, in February seventeen eighty-five, terrible news arrived.
His father had died of stomach cancer.
The family was left with very little money, and the young cadet took on the job of looking after them.
He raced through the Paris course in a single year, when most students needed two or three.
In September seventeen eighty-five, aged just sixteen, he became a second lieutenant of artillery.
But a lieutenant earned very little, and in the king's army, promotions went to men with connections.
He could easily have spent his whole life as a forgotten junior officer.
Then, in seventeen eighty-nine, France exploded.
